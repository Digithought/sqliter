# Why

Every benchmark in the suite is timed by the same recipe: a fixed number of warmup runs
then a fixed number of timed runs, with the numbers written by hand into each benchmark
definition. Today that means 3 and 10 by default, or whatever the suite author guessed.
Two things go wrong, both measured during planning on an isolated harness.

**Too few samples on the fast benchmarks, so the median wanders.** `execution/full-scan-10k`
at the current 2 warmups and 10 iterations, run four times each in a fresh process,
produced medians of **14.67, 14.20, 20.90 and 25.44 ms** — a 1.8x spread with nothing
changed between runs. `execution/group-by-10k` gave **52.21, 51.53, 81.45 and 50.89 ms**.
Raising the sample count helps: `full-scan-10k` at 20 warmups and 100 iterations gave
**12.43, 12.47 and 20.06 ms**, and `group-by-10k` at 10 and 40 gave **60.00, 65.03 and
58.22 ms** — tighter, though not cured (see the honest limit below).

**Below a millisecond, the clock is most of the measurement.** The parser benchmarks
measure 14-84 microseconds per iteration. Fifty iterations of a 14-microsecond function is
0.7 ms of total timed work, sampled through `performance.now()` one call at a time. That
number is dominated by timer resolution and scheduler jitter, not by the parser.

**And nothing reports how noisy any of it was.** The harness prints median, p95, min and
max. On a 10-iteration benchmark `percentile(arr, 95)` indexes `ceil(0.95 * 10) - 1 = 9`,
the last element — so **the p95 column is literally the max column** for every benchmark in
the suite except the four parser ones. Two columns, one number, and no measure of spread
anywhere.

# What to build

## Calibrate each benchmark from a pilot run

Replace the fixed `warmup` / `iterations` defaults with a duration-driven procedure in the
worker. After the benchmark's `setup`:

1. **Warm up by duration.** Run `fn` until either a warmup time target is met or a cap is
   hit: `warmupIterations = clamp(ceil(warmupTargetMs / pilotDurationMs), 2, 50)` with
   `warmupTargetMs = 250`. Take `pilotDurationMs` from a single timed call made *after* at
   least two untimed calls, so the pilot is not itself the cold first run.
2. **Choose an inner batch size** so one sample is comfortably above clock resolution:
   `batch = clamp(ceil(minSampleMs / pilotDurationMs), 1, 10000)` with `minSampleMs = 1`.
   A sample times `batch` consecutive `fn` calls and divides by `batch`.
3. **Choose a sample count** from a total-time target:
   `samples = clamp(ceil(targetTotalMs / (batch * pilotDurationMs)), minSamples, maxSamples)`
   with `targetTotalMs = 1000`, `minSamples = 5`, `maxSamples = 500`.
4. **Stop early on a total-time ceiling.** Once `minSamples` have been collected and
   elapsed timed work exceeds `maxTotalMs = 5000`, stop.

Put these five constants in one exported object with a comment explaining what each one
trades away, so tuning is a one-line edit rather than an archaeology exercise.

Worked expectations against the planning measurements, as a sanity check on the constants:

| benchmark | per-iteration | batch | samples | timed total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| parser/simple-select | 14 us | 72 | 500 | ~0.5 s |
| execution/full-scan-10k | ~13 ms | 1 | 77 | ~1.0 s |
| execution/group-by-10k | ~60 ms | 1 | 17 | ~1.0 s |
| execution/order-by-text-prefix40-10k | ~74 ms | 1 | 14 | ~1.0 s |
| mutation/bulk-insert-10k | ~190 ms | 1 | 6 | ~1.1 s |
| mutation/single-row-insert-1k | ~135 ms | 1 | 8 | ~1.1 s |

An explicit `iterations` / `warmup` on a benchmark still wins and skips calibration
entirely; the result records that the benchmark was pinned rather than calibrated, so a
reader can tell the difference.

## Remove the hand-tuned counts from the four suite files

The existing `iterations` and `warmup` values were chosen for the fixed-count model and
are now the thing preventing calibration from working. Delete them from all 27 benchmark
definitions. Keep the override *mechanism* and document it in the suite files' header
comments as an escape hatch for a benchmark that genuinely needs pinning — but do not leave
any benchmark pinned "just in case", because a pinned benchmark opts out of the stability
reporting below.

## Report the spread

Compute a dispersion figure per benchmark and print it as a column beside the median.

**Use relative interquartile range** — `(p75 - p25) / median`, printed as a percentage.
Chosen over coefficient of variation because these distributions carry occasional
garbage-collection and tier-up outliers that inflate a standard deviation without telling
you anything about the typical iteration; the planning data shows rIQR discriminating well,
with `group-by-10k` reading 9.1% and 11.8% on its two well-behaved passes and **63.2%** on
the pass whose median was off. Record the choice and this reasoning in a comment at the
computation, so the next person does not swap it back on aesthetic grounds.

Replace the p95 column with the spread column. p95 duplicates max at every realistic sample
count and its slot is better spent. Keep median, min, max, and add spread; keep the raw
timings out of the table but record `samples`, `batch` and `pinned` in the results JSON
alongside them so a later analysis is possible without re-running.

## Mark unstable benchmarks

A benchmark whose own within-run rIQR exceeds **20%** cannot support a claim about a change
smaller than 20%, so it is not a usable gate. Flag it in the table (an `unstable` marker on
the row) and set a per-benchmark `stable: false` in the results JSON. This ticket only
*reports* the flag; the downstream comparison ticket is what excludes unstable benchmarks
from pass/fail.

**Be honest about what this does and does not catch.** Planning found `full-scan-10k`
producing a 20.06 ms median with an rIQR of only 8.8%, in a run whose two neighbours read
12.4 ms — a tight distribution around a wrong centre. Within-run dispersion catches
within-run noise; it does not catch process-level or machine-level drift. Write that
limitation into a `NOTE:` at the stability check so nobody reads a low spread as a
guarantee of comparability. Environment capture and the comparison rules that partly
address it are the next ticket.

# Edge cases & interactions

- **A pilot that measures zero.** `performance.now()` can return the same value twice for a
  trivial `fn`. Clamp `pilotDurationMs` to a small positive floor before dividing, or batch
  size and sample count both go to infinity.
- **A median of zero.** Sub-rounding-floor timings must not produce `NaN` or `Infinity` in
  the spread column. `checkRatioGuards` already handles a zero median explicitly; the
  dispersion math needs the same treatment — report the spread as unknown, mark the
  benchmark unstable, and never emit a non-finite number into the results JSON.
- **Batching changes what min and max mean.** For a batched benchmark each sample is an
  average over `batch` calls, so min and max are batch averages, not true per-iteration
  extremes. Say so in the table legend and in the results JSON field names, or the numbers
  quietly lie about tail behaviour.
- **Batching requires `fn` to be repeatable without `setup` between calls.** It already is
  for all 27 — the current harness runs 10 back-to-back — but a future benchmark could
  break it. Note the requirement in the suite header comments.
- **A benchmark whose cost changes as it runs.** `mutation/update-where-1k` runs an update
  and then a reset, so it is self-balancing; a benchmark that grew a table each call would
  make the pilot unrepresentative and the median meaningless. None exists today; if the
  audit finds one, pin it and say why.
- **`maxTotalMs` must never cut below `minSamples`.** Check the sample count first, the
  elapsed ceiling second, in that order.
- **Wall-clock budget.** The prereq puts a full run near 33 s. Calibration targets ~1 s of
  timed work per benchmark against today's mixed 0.7 ms - 1.9 s, so expect the total to
  land somewhere around 60-90 s. Measure it and report the number. If it exceeds three
  minutes, lower `targetTotalMs` rather than reintroducing hand-tuned counts.
- **The ratio guard's inputs both move.** `correlated-subquery` and
  `hand-batched-peer-count` get new sample counts, so re-confirm the guard passes and still
  fails when deliberately broken, exactly as the prereq ticket did.

# Acceptance

- Repeating `yarn bench --filter full-scan-10k` five times produces medians whose spread is
  visibly tighter than the 14.20-25.44 ms range measured at the fixed counts. State the five
  numbers in the handoff. Do not claim the problem is solved if it is not — the honest
  result may be "tighter, with one outlier run", which is what planning saw.
- The parser benchmarks report a batch size above 1 and several hundred samples.
- Every row carries a spread figure; no row shows `NaN`, `Infinity`, or an empty cell.
- No benchmark definition in the four suite files carries `iterations` or `warmup` unless a
  comment says why.
- Full-run wall-clock is measured and reported.
